The FPUT Problem Explained

This simulation is a browser port of a Fortran FPUT solver: the same force law — F = k(y′ + αy′²), a spring with a small quadratic correction — and the same fourth-order Yoshida symplectic integrator, just running client-side instead of from the command line. Excite the chain in a single low-frequency mode, watch the energy chart below it, and you're watching the same recurrence Fermi, Pasta, Ulam, and Tsingou first saw on the MANIAC computer in 1955: a nonlinear system that should drift toward equal energy in every mode, but instead keeps almost returning to where it started.

In Loving Memory of Mary Tsingou

In the summer of 1953, Enrico Fermi, John Pasta, Stanislaw Ulma and Mary Tsingou set out to study the behavior of a chain of masses on weakly nonlinear springs. They published their results in a Los Alamos report in 1955. The resulting phenomenon, named the Fermi–Pasta–Ulam problem, notably ignored the contributions of Mary Tsingou. However, in 2008, Thierry Dauxois published an article in Physics Today that highlighted her contributions to the problem. Follwing this, the problem was renamed to the Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou problem in her honor.

Mary Tsingou earned her undergradute degree from the University of Wisconsin and a Master's degree in Applied Mathematics from the University of Michigan. In 1952, she joined the Los Alamos National Laboratory, where she dedicated her entire 35-year career. Selected as one of the first programmers for the historic MANIAC computer, she spent her career solving complex problems in mathematics and applied physics. During her time at Los Alamos, she made significant contributions to the FRUT promlem, the Star Wars program, and was a early expert in Fortran.

As one of the first people to use computers to study complex systems, and her work on the FRUT problem helped to lay the foundation for the field of chaos theory. Today, we remember Mary Tsingou for her pioneering work in the field of nonlinear dynamics and her many contributions in computing. This website is dedicated in her memory.
